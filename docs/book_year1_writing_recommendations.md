# Year 1 Book: How It Should Be Written

Team recommendations, 2026-10-07, merged from five memos in
`C:\Projects\books\BHSR_Year1_Illustrated\recommendations\` (structure, voice, accuracy, images, reader).
**Adopted 2026-10-07** as the Year 1 writing guide: `docs/book_series_playbook.md` points every agent here. Where
this guide and the author's decisions at the end differ, the decisions win.

## The short version

Write it like a careful investigator with a Bible open, not like the videos. Each chapter asks one plain question,
follows real clues, says how sure the answer is, and ends on Christ. Quote less, source everything, and let the
mysteries carry the drama.

## 1. Voice (writer, reader)

- "We" narrates and "you" points the reader at the page. No "I" in chapters: a pen name can't tell personal
  stories without inventing them.
- Reading level grade 8–10: one idea per sentence, mostly 12–22 words, a short sentence to land each point.
- Investigator, not prosecutor: give each reading, who holds it, and how far the evidence leans.
- Name the source in the sentence: "Genesis says… 1 Enoch adds… later rabbis taught… some modern writers propose."
- On a source's first mention in each chapter, give one phrase of who and when ("Augustine, the fifth-century bishop
  of Hippo"; "the Mishnah, the code of Jewish law compiled about AD 200"). Chapters are read a week apart and
  excerpted alone, so repeat the phrase in every chapter. (Adopted from the pilot review, 2026-10-08.)
- Full forms by default. A contraction may land a short sentence where the full form sounds stiff: two or three per
  chapter at most, none in The Answer or the "This book…" paragraph. (Pilot review.)
- Print vocabulary: "proves" → "supports", "confirms" → "agrees with", "decoded" → "read closely". No "shocking",
  "chilling", "bombshell", no capitals for emphasis; "exact" only for numbers that are in the text.
- Take the tone of the community posts ("You were not an afterthought"), not the reel hype ("Never Taught in
  Church").
- Application stays to two or three sentences inside the Christological key, not a sermon close.
- `voice.md` has a 300-word sample opening of Chapter 1 in this voice, plus a before/after of a W01 script passage.

## 2. Chapter shape (about 2,500 words) (architect, writer, reader)

| Piece | Words | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opener page | — | Number, title as a plain question (8 words or fewer), one-line mystery question, key reference, opener image |
| Scene hook | ~250 | Must stand alone (Amazon "Look Inside", excerpts) |
| 3–5 clue sections | ~1,700 | H2s named for the evidence ("The Waters Already There"), never "Clue 1"; five fits the weekday reading plan |
| The Answer | ~300 | Says how sure it is |
| The Key | ~250 | Christological; rests on a New Testament text, not on the contested claim |
| Bridge | 1–2 lines | The page-turn to the next chapter |
| Notes | — | Numbered endnotes |

- Vary the openers (a verse read slowly, two verses that clash, a scene, an artifact, a question) so neighbouring
  chapters don't feel like a template.
- Boxes, at most one of each per chapter: a Hebrew/Greek Insight (60–120 words); a "Weighing the Evidence" box
  (100 words or less) only where a claim is debated.
- Outside the callout, at most two transliterated Hebrew or Greek words per section, each glossed on first use.
  Further words go to the notes or are cut. (Pilot review.)
- The opener lines and opener type come from the chapter's brief in `outline.md`; no two chapters within three
  places of each other share an opener type. (Pilot review.)
- One pull-quote of 200 characters or less per chapter; marketing reuses it.

## 3. Scripture and sources (editor, writer)

- Quote 4–6 load-bearing passages per chapter (300–400 words); cite the rest by reference. Re-take every quote from
  the KJV text: the scripts quote modern copyrighted wording (W01's Psalm 74 and Job 26 match the NIV).
- Quotation marks only for exact wording from a named edition.
- When one passage is linked to another, words of purpose ("God designed", "Jesus gives", "on purpose", "a type
  of") go only with links the Bible itself states or draws. Every other echo is "heard", "read as" or "seen by
  readers". (Pilot review; follows the author's general stance.)
- Notes in Chicago short form. Ancient texts by standard reference (Josephus, *Ant.* 1.73; m. Yoma 6:8); Dead Sea
  Scrolls by siglum, column and line (11Q13 ii 6–9), with date of composition kept apart from date of the copy.
- A claim found only in a script is unsourced: source it, cut it, or recast it as "a popular tradition holds".
  Never cite the video.
- Recompute every number and show the working in a note. Example: W52's 1,500 miles assumes a 660-ft furlong; the
  606-ft stadion the script names gives about 1,380.
- Per chapter: the writer lists claims by type (Scripture, ancient text, archaeology/number, interpretation) and
  verifies each; the editor checks every quote, reference and number and opens three cited sources.

## 4. The "unseen realm" chapters (editor)

W01, W06–W08, W12–W13, W25, W41, W49–W50: the text first, then the ancient reading, then the debate.
- The angelic reading of Genesis 6 is presented as the oldest Jewish and Christian one (1 Enoch, Josephus, echoed in
  Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4), with the Sethite and royal readings given fairly.
- Psalm 82's *elohim* are created beings; Yahweh is unique. Jude quotes 1 Enoch the way Paul quotes Aratus.
- Credit Michael Heiser for "the Deuteronomy 32 worldview" and explain the 32:8 manuscript variant.
- Stop where the text stops: no DNA, CRISPR, chimeras or portals.

## 5. Riskiest weeks (editor)

| Week | Script claim | Book treatment |
|---|---|---|
| W06–W07, W09 | Watchers corrupted the genome; *tamim* = genetic purity | Enoch's story as Enoch's; *tamim* is also moral integrity (Gen 17:1) |
| W08 | Azazel "proven" a demon | Three views; the crimson thread is rabbinic tradition (m. Yoma 6:8) |
| W25 | Rujm el-Hiri a Rephaim portal | Excavators' dates; Ugaritic Rephaim texts say nothing of height |
| W26 | Archaeology "proves" Jericho | Garstang, Kenyon, Wood and radiocarbon as a live debate |
| W33–W34, W39 | Carbon-14 "proof"; Septuagint "250 BC"; Jesus "cites" 4Q521 | Published date ranges; Septuagint Isaiah is 2nd century BC; shared Isaiah 61 background |
| W36–W37 | 1948 fulfils Ezekiel 37; 173,880 days to Palm Sunday | The main prophetic readings; Anderson's and Hoehner's different dates |
| W40, W44 | Kepler conjunction; Thallus and Phlegon "confirm" the darkness | Rival star theories; both survive only in later quotes; a Passover full moon rules out a solar eclipse |
| W41, W43 | Tabor a Roman fort; crucifixion on the Mount of Olives | Josephus fortified Tabor himself; the Olives site is a minority view |
| W52 | Hypercube; gemstone "proof"; "Dr. Max Neunhöffer" | Revelation says cube; cut the untraceable source |

Also avoid: letter and name codes as proof (*Bereshit* "spells" the cross), current politics as fulfilment, straw
men ("pulpits treat Genesis 6 like an embarrassing secret"), absolutes ("every single time").

## 6. Structure (architect)

- Four parts, four kinds of looking, mirroring the introduction: I reaching (Eden, the Watchers, Babel); II looking
  back (Abram leaves; Lot's wife); III looking into hidden things (the Ark, the scrolls); IV the veil torn and the
  open face (Revelation 22:4). Revelation's Tree of Life closes the loop Eden opens.
- Year 1 is two books (Parts I–II = Book One, Parts III–IV = Book Two). Each book has its own introduction
  (about 1,500–2,000 words) and conclusion (about 1,000–1,500). The cat saying opens Book One's introduction and is
  completed in Book Two's conclusion; see "Book theme" in the playbook.
- Part openers: two pages — a map or timeline, a ~200-word essay on that part's "look", and the part's mystery
  questions as a mini table of contents. No part closers.
- Back matter, in each book: one-spread timeline, glossary (~25–30 terms), study guide, Scripture index, credits.
  Four maps, all first used in Book One: nations and Babel (W12–13), Abraham's route (W14), Hermon–Bashan
  (W06/W25, reused W41), Jerusalem–Moriah (W17, reused W28/W45). Book Two reprints the two it needs.
- One chapter owns each repeated topic: W01 the divine council, W03 the Eden cherub, W06 Watchers/Hermon, W07 the
  Book of Giants, W08 Azazel, W13 Deuteronomy 32, W49 the Babel reversal. Others get two sentences and "see
  Chapter N" (across books: "see Book One, Chapter 13"). Book Two must still make sense to a reader who skipped
  Book One: give the one-paragraph version before pointing back.
- Page budget (estimate, not measured): about 240–270 pages per book. Test-build three Book One chapters first
  (W01, a full-script week with art such as W12, and a thin week such as W20).

## 7. Images (art director)

- Three tiers, never mixed within a tier:
  - Chapter openers: one locked AI house style, **rendered new in portrait**. Existing scene art is landscape
    1344 x 768, which prints at about 90 DPI cropped to a full page.
  - Inline: real evidence (manuscripts, artifacts, sites, maps) plus Doré and Tissot scenes.
  - Part openers: maps and timelines.
- Captions by tier: evidence (object, place, date, museum, credit); art ("Gustave Doré, *The Deluge*, 1866"); AI
  ("Illustration (AI-generated): …"). Refer to figures by number, never "above" or "below" (ebooks reflow).
- Reject existing frames with baked-in lettering or anachronisms (W25 `scene_12` "REIPHAM", W01 `scene_20`
  garbled "TOHU", Viking horns in W11): re-render, don't retouch. Lift dark frames; W11's print near-black.
- No faces of Christ or God, no photoreal "proof" of contested claims; AI never stands in for an artifact that
  exists.
- Also needed: divine council and 70 nations diagram, seed-line strip Adam to Christ, Tabernacle and Temple plans at
  one scale, Daniel's 70 weeks timeline, scroll timeline and Qumran map, Isaiah 7:14 Hebrew/Greek side by side,
  New Jerusalem cube against the Holy of Holies to scale.
- Reality: only 13 of 52 weeks have usable chapter art; 35 need new renders or other sources.

## 8. Reader and shareability (strategist)

- The reader: a churchgoing adult who loves a mystery and wants receipts.
- Chapter titles are plain questions, 8 words or fewer, different from the video titles.
- Reading rhythm: one chapter a week, 4–5 sections that fit Monday to Friday alongside the reels; each book is a
  half-year reading plan.
- Write each book's introduction and first chapter last: "Look Inside" shows them, and W01 carries the most
  contested material.
- Disclose the AI art in the credits; being found out costs more trust than saying so.

## 9. Sources and order of work (architect, verified)

- Year 1 has 24 full scripts, 12 thin or boilerplate ones (W04, W07, W10, W13, W14, W17, W19–W24) and 16 weeks with
  none. About 28 chapters need real research.
- Book One (W01–W26) is far better sourced, so it is written first. Book Two's 15 missing weeks are researched
  meanwhile, starting with W44–W49 (the darkness, the veil, "It is finished", the resurrection, Emmaus, Pentecost),
  its climax.
- A thin chapter at 2,000 words beats one padded with the boilerplate paragraph.

## Where the team disagrees

1. **Discussion questions.** The strategist wants a ~100-word "Talk it over" at each chapter's end; the architect
   wants them in a back-of-book study guide so the bridge line is the last thing read. Lean: the back of the book,
   unless small-group leaders are the main buyer.
2. **Length vs pages.** Resolved by splitting Year 1 into two books: about 2,500 words, up to 3,000 where the
   script is full, thin chapters shorter; about 240–270 pages per book.
3. **Title.** The strategist (opinion, not market-tested) would move "Curiosity Killed the Cat" to the
   introduction and back cover and change the main title, since it carries no Bible signal for Amazon search and
   may read as a warning to cautious buyers. (Decided: *Holy Curiosity*, now a two-book series.) Alternatives were:
   *Holy Curiosity: 52 Hidden Secrets of the Bible*;
   *Search Out the Matter* (Proverbs 25:2); *Things Angels Desire to Look Into* (1 Peter 1:12). Search Amazon for
   each before choosing.

## Author's decisions

Decided 2026-10-07:
- Year 1 is **two books** in the series **Holy Curiosity**: Book One W01–W26 (Creation to Jericho), Book Two
  W27–W52 (Goliath to the New Jerusalem). "Curiosity killed the cat" opens Book One; Book Two's conclusion
  completes the saying.
- Debated questions (angels in Genesis 6, 1948, W04's "6,000 years"): each view fairly, then Ezra Keys' leaning and
  why.
- Discussion questions: a study guide at the back (two per chapter plus a reading plan), none at chapter ends.
- Jesus, God and angels: silhouettes, light or symbols only; no faces.
- Chapter length: about 2,500 words, up to 3,000 where the script is full; thin-source chapters may run shorter
  rather than be padded.
- Ezra Keys' general stance (from the pilot answers, 2026-10-08): go as far as Scripture goes and no further.
  Claim as God's design only what Scripture itself states or draws; present everything beyond that as a reading.
  In the text the view is stated as "This book…" / "we", never "I". Pilot views: Chapter 5, the blood reading
  (Genesis 3:21, Hebrews 9:22); Chapter 12, Babel in the land of Shinar, no ruin named; Chapter 20, only the
  Joseph patterns the New Testament draws (Acts 7; Genesis 50:20 with Acts 2:23).

Still open:
1. W01's picture-letter "cross" and the pre-cosmic fall: drop, or keep as a labelled devotional reading in a box.
2. AI chapter openers (disclosed), and color or black & white (needed before openers are rendered).
3. KJV only, or the WEB where the KJV hides the point (Isaiah 45:18's "in vain" is *tohu*)?
4. May Ezra Keys say "I" in the introduction and conclusion?
5. Rename Part II "Patriarchs, Exodus & the Promised Land" (W25–W26 are the conquest)?
6. Can a theologian or Hebrew/Greek reader review the riskiest weeks before production?
7. Main buyer: fan keepsake or small-group leader? YouTube/Facebook audience data would settle the
   devotional-vs-academic balance.

The editor flagged three of its own points as unverified; check before use: the "two hundred donkeys" line's
source (1Q23 vs 4Q531), b. Hullin 90b on the veil's thickness, and whether "Dr. Max Neunhöffer" exists at all.
